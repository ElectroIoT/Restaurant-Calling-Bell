Restaurant Calling Bell: full plan

A guest presses one button; the counter shows the table number on an OLED, says "Table 3 is calling for service", repeats until staff press ACK, and the table's remote knows its call was received. Built on ATmega328P + SI24R1, open source, designed for your own PCB layout in EasyEDA Pro.

Where it stands (2026-10-29): firmware written and compiling, protocol tested in software, parts looked up on LCSC. No hardware has been powered yet. Repo: github.com/ElectroIoT/Restaurant-Calling-Bell.

1. How it works

Table remote

ATmega328P + SI24R1, CR2450 or 2xAAA. Sleeps at about 3 uA (estimate). Press: call, retries with the same sequence number, waits for the receiver's reply, lights solid on success, flashes 5 times on failure.

CALL→←CALL_ACCEPTED

Counter receiver

ATmega328P + SI24R1 + SSD1306 + DFPlayer Mini. Always listening. Validates, stores, replies, shows, announces, repeats every 30 s until ACK. Pending calls survive a power cut.

Why this is more reliable than "radio ACK": the radio's automatic ACK only says a radio heard the frame. This design sends a separate reply from the receiver firmware after it recorded the call, and the remote counts the call as accepted only on that reply. A lost reply makes the remote retry; the receiver recognises the retry (same table, epoch, sequence) and answers again without creating a second entry.

3. Hardware plan

Remote board

  • ATmega328P-AU, 4 MHz from the 8 MHz RC (fuse CKDIV8 + /2 in firmware): rated down to 1.8 V
  • SI24R1 module at the board edge, antenna hanging off the edge
  • CALL tact switch + 2-pin header for a big panel button; red LED
  • Supply direct from CR2450 or 2xAAA, no regulator
  • 100 nF + 22 uF at the radio, ceramic only
  • 470 k external pull-up: stuck button costs about 6 uA
  • ISP header, test points, 4 mounting holes
  • 4 x 0 ohm jumpers to choose holder polarity

Receiver board

  • ATmega328P-AU, 8 MHz crystal, 3.3 V logic
  • USB-C power only, PTC, TVS, AP2112K-3.3 LDO
  • SI24R1 module at the edge, antenna clear
  • 0.96in SSD1306 OLED header, 3 buttons (ACK, CLEAR, MUTE) + reset
  • DFPlayer Mini sockets (5 V), speaker screw terminal, active buzzer
  • DFPlayer on the hardware UART, ISP programming
  • 5 V bulk 220 uF + 2 x 10 uF for speaker peaks

Power facts to respect

  • Radio 1.9-3.6 V: never fresh lithium AAA (3.6 V)
  • 12 mA TX pulses at 0 dBm, 16 mA at 4 dBm
  • ATmega328P: 8 MHz needs 2.7 V, 4 MHz works to 1.8 V
  • No charger on a primary cell, ever
  • Speaker: start at volume 20, check the amplifier temperature

5. Protocol in one screen

packet (8 bytes): [ver|type][table][seq][epoch][status][battery x20mV][flags][crc8] types: 1 CALL 2 CALL_ACCEPTED 3 CALL_SERVED 4 ERROR 5 STATUS_REQ addresses: receiver "REST1" remote n "TAB01".."TAB10" channel 76 (2476 MHz), 250 kbps, 0 dBm remote: CALL(seq=17) ... no reply in 40 ms ... backoff 60..180 ms ... CALL(seq=17) again (max 8) receiver: same (table, epoch, seq) = duplicate: reply again, never a second entry new call: store, show, announce, reply ACCEPTED, then save to EEPROM remote: after ACCEPTED sleeps; every 8 s for 5 min asks STATUS_REQ; blinks 3x on SERVED; UNKNOWN_CALL (receiver lost it in a power cut) = re-send the same call

Details, state machines and the Mermaid diagrams: docs/wireless-protocol.md, hardware/diagrams/*.mmd.

7. My advice

1. Battery

Fit 2 x AAA unless your sag measurement shows the CR2450 stays above 2.2 V during a 12 mA pulse. The estimate says a CR2450 is fine when fresh but loses its last part of capacity to voltage sag, and an AAA pair has far lower internal resistance. The PCB takes either on the same rail.

2. Prototype first

Run two Uno boards and two modules until tests 2-9 pass. Many SI24R1 modules need a separate 3.3 V source with 22 uF at the pins; flaky links are almost always supply noise.

3. Antenna

Hang the module's antenna end off the PCB edge and keep the coin holder, battery, metal case and speaker wires 10 mm away. A detuned antenna looks like "short range".

4. Real hardware checks before layout

Measure your DFPlayer (row spacing, pin order), OLED (pin order), the SI24R1 module pad pitch and the holder polarity. The footprints in the guide are from drawings and from memory.

5. Guest button

A bigger button is a better guest experience: use the EXT BUTTON header and a panel-mount button in a table stand. Keep the PCB switch for testing.

6. Voice

Record the 14 files in a real voice at consistent volume; one 1.5 s announcement is easier to understand than a long sentence in a noisy restaurant. Keep the repeat interval long enough that staff are not nagged (30 s default, 5 repeats).

7. Channel and Wi-Fi

Default channel 76 (2476 MHz) sits above Wi-Fi channels 1/6/11. A router on channel 13 overlaps it. Check with a Wi-Fi analyser at the venue and change BELL_CHANNEL if test 15 fails.

8. Power cuts

The receiver restores pending calls from EEPROM. Add a small USB power bank in line if the restaurant has frequent cuts.

9. Later ideas

An ESP32 bridge on the receiver's spare UART could forward calls to RestoProERP or a phone. Keep it out of v1: it adds a failure mode to the part that must never fail.

10. Compliance

0 dBm on 2.4 GHz is normally licence-free, but check your country's rules for the module you buy. Do not sell units without checking.

8. Risks that can still bite

  • Unproven on hardware: radio driver, DFPlayer commands, OLED, sleep current, range.
  • Coin cell sag at end of life can reset the remote or stop the radio.
  • Footprint assumptions (DFPlayer spacing, holder polarity, crystal load capacitance, 220 uF body size, PTC and 1N4148W availability).
  • Save window: a power cut within about 10-50 ms after a reply can lose a call; the remote's polling recovers it, but only while polling is on.
  • Two remotes with the same table id answer each other's replies: label and verify each unit.
